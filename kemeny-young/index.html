Kemeny-Young Ranking

A consensus method from social choice theory that combines multiple rankings into one mathematically optimal result

What Is Kemeny-Young Ranking?

A Voting Problem

Each of the 6 clinical use cases produces its own ranking of the 15 models. Melanoma might rank Mistral Small first; Lung Cancer might prefer Llama-4 Maverick. How do you combine these into one fair consensus ranking? That's the ranking aggregation problem.

Minimize Disagreement

Kemeny-Young finds the consensus ranking by brute force: it checks every possible ordering of models and scores each one against what the use cases actually said. Here's how:

  1. Take a candidate ranking, e.g. Mistral > Maverick > Qwen > DeepSeek.
  2. Look at every pair implied by that ranking: Mistral > Maverick, Mistral > Qwen, Maverick > Qwen, etc. (6 pairs for 4 models).
  3. For each pair, count how many use cases agree. If 2 of 3 use cases also rank Mistral above Maverick, that pair scores 2.
  4. Sum the scores across all pairs — that's the total pairwise agreement for this candidate.
  5. Repeat for every possible ordering (4! = 24 candidates). The one with the highest total agreement wins.

The winning ranking disagrees with the fewest individual use-case judgements — that's what makes it the mathematically optimal consensus.

Explain It Like I'm 5

Imagine you and two friends each rank your favourite ice cream flavours. You say: chocolate, vanilla, strawberry. Friend 1 says: vanilla, chocolate, strawberry. Friend 2 says: chocolate, strawberry, vanilla.

Now you need to pick ONE ranking everyone can live with. You look at every pair: "chocolate vs vanilla" — 2 out of 3 people put chocolate first, so chocolate wins that matchup. Do that for every pair, and pick the ranking that agrees with the most matchups. That's Kemeny-Young: the ranking that makes the fewest people grumble.

In the paper, "people" are clinical use cases (melanoma, lung cancer, etc.) and "ice cream flavours" are AI models.

Historical Context & Importance

Kemeny-Young ranking emerged from social choice theory, the mathematical study of voting and group decisions. It was developed to address a fundamental problem: when multiple people rank alternatives differently, how do you aggregate their preferences fairly? Naive approaches (like averaging scores) can lead to paradoxes where the consensus ranking violates transitivity or contradicts unanimous pairwise preferences. Kemeny-Young solves this by finding the ranking that minimizes total pairwise disagreement — a mathematically optimal consensus that respects the structure of the underlying votes.

How This Applies to the Paper

The paper applied Kemeny-Young ranking to combine individual use case rankings into one consensus ranking of all 15 models. Instead of averaging scores (which can lead to paradoxes), Kemeny-Young finds the single ranking that minimizes total disagreement across the 6 clinical use cases.

Result: Mistral Small 3.1 24B achieved the #1 consensus rank despite being only the #2 model by mean macro-average. This ranking is mathematically optimal for the given data, not arbitrary.

The actual paper runs on all 15 models × 6 use cases (the calculator above uses 4 models × 3 use cases to keep it interactive), but the principle is identical: find the consensus that offends the least.

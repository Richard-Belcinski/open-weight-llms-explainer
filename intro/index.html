Evaluating Open-Weight LLMs for Medical Report Extraction

Spaanderman et al. benchmarked 15 open-weight LLMs across 6 prompting strategies on ~4,060 clinical reports from 3 institutions in 3 languages — all prompting, no fine-tuning.

The task: extract structured clinical variables (tumor grade, histological subtype, lesion dimensions, resection margins, etc.) directly from free-text narrative reports — turning unstructured prose into computable data, with no manual annotation step.

What Did They Do?

Scale

15 open-weight LLMs tested across 6 prompting strategies on 6 clinical use cases with ~4,060 reports and 73 extracted variables.

Approach

Compared LLM performance against inter-rater agreement (IRA). Used consensus ranking (Kemeny-Young) to identify best model-strategy combinations. All prompting — no fine-tuning.

Why This Matters

Prompting vs Fine-Tuning

This is the prompting-only counterpart to the Strata fine-tuning approach

Model Selection

Small general-purpose models (24B) match large ones (685B) with optimized prompting

Medical Models Underperform

Med42, OpenBioLLM, MedGemma all worse than general-purpose models for extraction

Clinical-Grade Results

LLM performance closely aligned with human inter-rater agreement

Cost Tradeoff

Best prompting strategy is 8.4x slower than the practical alternative (few-shot)

Open-Weight LLMs for Medical Report Extraction

Spaanderman et al. | arXiv 2511.10658 | Interactive Explainer

Critiques & Discussion

5 strengths, 8 critiques, and 5 discussion questions for journal club

Strengths

Scale and rigor

15 models, 6 prompting strategies, 6 clinical use cases, 3 institutions, 3 languages, ~4,060 reports

Statistical methods

Bootstrap CIs, linear mixed-effects modeling, Kemeny-Young rank aggregation — stronger framework than most comparable studies

Clinically grounded

Compares LLM scores against inter-rater agreement (IRA) — several tasks show LLMs exceeding IRA

Practical finding

Mistral Small 24B ranked highest overall — small general-purpose models match large models with optimized prompting

Open-source code

Code released via Zenodo for reproducibility

Discussion Points

What would results look like with LoRA fine-tuning on 200-300 examples per use case?

Strata achieves 90% exact match with ≤100 training examples and fine-tuned 8B models.

How do these numbers compare to Strata's fine-tuned extraction results (which track P/R/F1)?

This study uses balanced accuracy while Strata reports exact match accuracy, precision, recall, and F1.

The unexplained variance problem: inherent to extraction tasks or a limitation of their analysis?

45-80% of performance variation is unexplained by model or strategy choice.

Is the prompt graph approach practical for production, or is few-shot the real winner?

Prompt graph: +12.7% improvement but 37.6 sec/report. Few-shot: +5.1% at 4.5 sec/report.

Why do medical-specialized models hurt performance on extraction tasks?

General models scored 0.77-0.78 while medical models scored 0.73 on average.
